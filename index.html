What it does

Your accounts, your investments and your plan in one place

Most people end up with three things. A record of what went in and out. A spreadsheet of investments. Another spreadsheet for the plan. WealthHorizon holds all three. Change something in one and the others change too.

The register

One row per transaction, with a running balance beside it. To check it against a statement, find the row where the two stop agreeing. Splits, transfers, payees, categories and repeating payments all work the way they did in Money.

Type a sum into an amount box and it works it out. 120+45.50+18 is fine.

Your investments

Holdings are priced in the currency they are quoted in. Every purchase is tracked on its own, so when you sell, it knows what that holding cost you. It handles share splits, dividends, corporate actions, foreign withholding tax, and the cash side of an investment account.

If a price is missing, it tells you. It does not quietly show you a figure that is too low.

What it comes to

One net worth figure, and a panel underneath showing the working. Every account, its balance, the rate it was converted at, and what that came to. If there is no rate for a currency, the figure stays in that currency. Nothing is guessed.

Snapshots record where you stood on a date. The chart shows what you had, not a fresh guess at it.

Editions

The four editions

Money Exporter — get the data out, and stop there if you want

Money Exporter is a program of its own. It reads your old file and writes it out in a format something else can open. You do not need the rest of WealthHorizon to use it.

It reads .mny files, password-protected ones included, as well as Access, QIF, OFX, QFX, QBO, CSV, Excel and text-based PDF statements. It writes CSV, Excel, QIF, OFX, QFX, QBO, IIF, GnuCash, YNAB and a PDF statement. It works out the format from the bytes in the file rather than the name on the end of it.

A .mny file says what each field is. A bank CSV says almost nothing, so some of it has to be worked out — which column is the amount, whether 01/02 is January or February, whether money out is a minus. Each guess is tested against the file’s own running balance, and it tells you whether it came back verified or not.

It is a Windows program and a command line tool, and it runs on your machine. Nothing is uploaded. There is a trial, then a one-off licence that does not expire.

Standard — what Microsoft Money did, without the Windows

Standard is meant as a straight swap. If you kept your accounts in Money, the things you used every day are here. Same shapes, same headings.

Your old file comes across. WealthHorizon opens .mny and .mdb/.accdb files directly, as well as QIF, OFX, CSV and Excel. Before it saves anything, it shows you what it found: every account, with the balance it worked out.

What Standard leaves out. The long-term planner, the month-end funding plan, share price forecasts, inheritance tax and the capital gains tools. Those are all in Pro. Nothing in Standard is cut down or time-limited. There is no cap on accounts, transactions or storage. The only limit is the size of your disk.

Pro — everything in Standard, and the questions Money could not answer

Money told you where you stood. Pro tells you where you are heading. Does the plan work? What does this month need? What might a holding be worth? What will the tax come to?

Long-term planning

Build a plan and see it year by year. Say what you want to live on. It works out how much of that your guaranteed income covers: state pension, defined benefit, earnings. Then it draws the rest from your pots, taking enough extra to pay the tax. It tells you the first year you fall short, when each pot runs out, and what is left at the end.

There are three outlooks and what-if scenarios. There is a Monte Carlo simulation, which gives you a range instead of one line. You decide the order you spend your pots in. That is a tax decision, not a sum.

Month-end planning

The month before it happens. One row per account: what it opens with, what comes in, what is due, its worst point, and whether it gets through. Then a worksheet of what to move from where. Each line says why that account was picked, and what else was considered. The transfers are grouped by the bank you log into, so you make one visit per login instead of one per payment.

Share price forecasting

A grid of what you expect each holding to be worth. Instruments down the side, dates across the top, a price in each cell. One growth rate across everything cannot tell one company from another. And an unlisted holding has no market price at all, so a forecast is the only price it will ever have. Import the spreadsheet you already keep, and export it back.

Inheritance tax and capital gains

An estate valuation with the nil-rate bands applied, and the sum shown line by line. A register of gifts against the seven-year rule. The options worth thinking about. For capital gains it uses FIFO, or the full UK matching rules: same day, then the next 30 days, then the pool. It brings in your unused losses and your allowance as well.

Advisory — a client book, and the records that go with it

The Advisory edition is for regulated firms. Every client gets the Pro product. The firm gets the layer above it. That means a management-information dashboard, a vulnerable customer register, fair value and charge assessments, suitability reporting, a firm hierarchy, and an audit trail nobody can edit.

It is built around the four outcomes in the FCA Consumer Duty: products and services, price and value, consumer understanding and consumer support. The Duty also asks a firm to watch for poor outcomes, act on them, and show that it did. That part is covered too.

The Advisory edition has its own section of this site, with detail on each module and screenshots of the advisory, compliance and firm-hierarchy views.

  • Consumer Duty MI dashboard — outcomes, vulnerability, charges, reviews and complaints across the book.

  • Vulnerability scoring — an explainable score per client, with the contributing factors listed.

  • Annual review tracker — overdue, due soon and on track, with the review recorded when it is done.
  • Risk assessment — an eight-question profile with a stated capacity for loss.
  • Suitability reports — COBS 9.4 letters with a reference, written from the client's file.
  • Board report — the annual attestation across the four Consumer Duty outcomes.
  • FCA evidence export — a structured supervisory pack in JSON and CSV.
  • Platform overview and firm hierarchy — adviser performance side by side, and rolled up through CEO, MD, VP, Manager and Adviser tiers.
  • Audit log — who looked at or changed what, and when. Only compliance can open it.

Money Exporter

A Money file you cannot open any more

Money Exporter opens it and saves it in a format something else can read. It is a separate program with its own licence. You can use it and never touch the rest of WealthHorizon.

Microsoft stopped selling Money in 2009. The file is still on your disk, and the figures are still in it. There is just less and less that will open it. Money Exporter reads .mny files, password-protected ones included. It also reads Access, QIF, OFX, QFX, QBO, CSV, Excel and PDF statements with real text in them.

It writes CSV, Excel, QIF, OFX, QFX, QBO, IIF, GnuCash, YNAB and a PDF statement. Splits stay split. Investments keep their lots and prices. You see what it found, account by account, before anything is written.

Where it has to guess, it tells you. A bank CSV does not label the amount column. It does not tell you whether 01/02 is January or February. So the program checks each guess against the running balance in the file. The answer comes back verified, partly verified, failed, or not available. “Not available” is never dressed up as verified.

It is a Windows program, with a command line tool beside it. It runs on your own machine, and nothing is uploaded.

Where it runs, and who can see it

Your data does not have to leave your building

You run it

You install every edition on your own hardware. The whole system is three containers: a database, an API and the web app. It will run on a laptop, a network drive at home, a server at the office, or any cloud you already use. There is nothing special about the hosting.

The only thing we run is a demonstration account, so you can look round before you install anything. The figures in it are made up. Your own records never touch our machines, on any edition.

It works offline

Market data, Open Banking and email are all optional. Set up none of them and it still works. Type prices and exchange rates in yourself, or import them. Nothing has to call home.

You can see who looked

If your account is linked to an adviser, the Account Access page lists who at the firm opened it, when, and in what role. Sharing your plan is a separate switch. You control it, and you can turn it off.

None of this is financial advice. The planning, tax and inheritance tax tools work out projections from what you type in. They are there to help you think. They are not advice, not a recommendation and not a guarantee. The application says so on the screens themselves: “Guidance, not advice. Check with an adviser before acting.” Tax rules change. Markets move. Your own position may turn on something the application cannot see. Get advice before you act on any of it.
