For a household

Personal finance tools

Most people arriving here have come from Microsoft Money and want the same thing again. There are three kinds of answer on the market. Desktop software you buy and install. Cloud services you subscribe to. Open-source projects you host yourself. Each trades something away.

Desktop software you install
Quicken Classic The best-known replacement for Money, and the closest thing to it in day-to-day use. Register, budgets, bills, investments. Installed on your own Windows or Mac machine. The file is yours, though the subscription has to stay current to keep using it. United States and Canada. Quicken says it does not sell outside those two countries, and its bank connections do not reach UK institutions. Retirement and lifetime planners, and US tax. Not UK tax, and no inheritance tax. Cloud services you subscribe to
PocketSmith Budgeting and cash-flow forecasting, with a calendar view and a long projection horizon. The vendor’s servers. Your transactions are held by them. Monarch Money A modern, well-made replacement for Quicken on the budgeting and net-worth side. The vendor’s servers. United States and Canada. No. Tracking rather than planning. Subscription.

GnuCash Double-entry accounting for personal and small-business use. Mature, and free. Your own machine. Open source. Worldwide. Several currencies. No. It is a ledger, and a good one. Free, open source.
Firefly III Self-hosted double-entry personal finance, with a strong rules engine for imports. A server you run. Open source. Worldwide. Several currencies in one instance. No. Free, open source.
Actual Budget Self-hosted envelope budgeting with a fast, modern interface. A server you run. Open source. Worldwide, with bank feeds through third-party services. No. Free, open source.
This one
WealthHorizon A register, a portfolio and a long-term plan in one product, with the tax and inheritance tax worked out year by year. Your own hardware, every edition. Three containers on a laptop, a server or your own cloud. We hold nothing. Anywhere. 198 currencies, each entry keeping its own rate and date, and more than one tax jurisdiction in the same household. Yes, in Pro. Real tax bands per year, capital gains, dividends, and inheritance tax for every year of the plan. Set out in full here. Free edition at 30 accounts, 1,000 transactions and 5 MB. No prices published for the paid editions yet.

The honest summary of that table. If you are in the United States, Quicken does more than we do and has done for thirty years. If you want a ledger and nothing else, Moneydance and GnuCash are good and we are more than you need. If you want somebody else to run it, we are the wrong answer, because we run nothing. Where we are worth a look is the combination: more than one currency, more than one country, and long-term tax and inheritance tax in the same product as the register, on a machine you own.

For an advice firm

Adviser and planning platforms

A UK advice firm does not buy one system. It buys a back office to hold the clients, a cashflow tool to do the planning, a risk-profiling tool, and increasingly something to produce Consumer Duty management information. Most firms run three or four products and pay to connect them. That is the market the Advisory edition sits in.

Platforms a UK advice firm already runs. Our reading of public information, October 2026.

Back office and client records
Intelliflo Office The most widely used adviser back office and CRM in the UK. Clients, cases, workflow, commission, reporting. Cloud, the vendor’s servers. Not its job. Firms connect a cashflow tool to it. Reporting and MI that a firm configures. A client portal.
Iress Xplan A large, configurable adviser platform used by bigger firms and networks. Cloud, the vendor’s servers. Modules available. Through configuration and reporting. A client portal.

Voyant The long-established UK cashflow modeller. Strong on complex and multi-generational cases. Cloud, the vendor’s servers. Yes, and it is the deepest of these. Widely regarded as the benchmark. Not its job. A client-facing view of the plan.
FE CashCalc A cashflow modeller with a gentler learning curve and clean client-facing output. Now part of the Dynamic Planner group. Cloud, the vendor’s servers. Yes, gross and net, with side-by-side scenarios. Templates and fact-find output. A client portal and fact-find.
Dynamic Planner Risk profiling first, with cashflow and suitability built around it. Heavily oriented to FCA expectations. Cloud, the vendor’s servers. Yes. Risk and suitability evidence rather than firm-wide outcome MI. A client questionnaire and report.

This one
WealthHorizon Advisory The client book, the planning, and the Consumer Duty evidence in one system, off one set of records. The firm’s own infrastructure — its premises, its private cloud, or a public one. The firm holds the data. Yes, the same engine the client uses, with real tax bands and inheritance tax year by year. Built in. Outcome MI, vulnerable customer register, fair value, charge justification, board report and an FCA evidence export. Mapped to the four outcomes here. The client’s own application, not a read-only portal. They keep their accounts in it, and choose what to share.

The honest summary of that table. Voyant does cashflow modelling better than we do, and has done it for longer. Intelliflo holds more of a firm’s working day than we do. If you have those two and they work, we are not a replacement for either. Two things are genuinely different here. The compliance evidence falls out of work already recorded instead of being assembled for a board meeting. And the client’s own records are the same records the adviser plans with, so there is nothing to reconcile and no second copy to keep in step.

Where the others are ahead of us

A comparison page that only lists what we win on is worth nothing. These are the reasons to pick something else, and they are real.

  • Bank feeds Quicken, PocketSmith, Monarch and the UK adviser platforms have working production bank connections. Ours runs against a provider’s sandbox. Production use needs regulatory permissions and provider onboarding that any account information service needs, and we do not have them yet. If you want your transactions to arrive on their own, today, we are the wrong choice. This is stated on the limitations list too.
  • Platform and provider integrations A UK advice firm expects valuations to arrive from the platforms it uses, and expects its back office to talk to them. The established vendors have spent years building those links. We have none of them. Data comes in by import, which is fine for a household and is work for a firm with hundreds of clients.
  • Track record and the size of the company Quicken has been sold since 1983. Voyant and Intelliflo have been in UK firms for many years and have support desks, training, user groups and a published roadmap. WealthHorizon is new, and Muneris is small. If buying from an established vendor matters to your firm, that is a legitimate reason to buy from one.
  • You have to run it Every edition installs on your own hardware. That is the point of it, and it is also a cost: somebody has to install it, back it up, keep it patched and keep the machine alive. A cloud service takes that away from you. We do not, and will not, because the moment we hold your records the main reason to use this is gone.
  • Depth of cashflow modelling Voyant models cases ours does not reach: complex multi-generational structures, trusts, and the long tail of situations a dedicated planning tool has met over fifteen years. Our planner is detailed about tax and inheritance tax and is honest about what it does not model.
  • Accessibility conformance We build to the WCAG 2.2 criteria for keyboard operability, visible focus and target size, and we test them. We have not commissioned an independent accessibility audit and we claim no formal conformance level. A firm with a procurement requirement for one should ask us before assuming.
  • Mobile applications There is no native iPhone or Android application. The web application is built for phones and tablets and reads offline, but it is a web application. Several of the products above have native apps.
