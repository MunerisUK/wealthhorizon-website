Alternatives

Where this one fits, and what it does not do

WealthHorizon is not the only way to do any of this, and for a lot of people it will not be the right way. This page says what kind of product it is, what it does not do, and what to go and check before you decide.

The shape of the market

Four kinds of product, and which one this is

Whatever you end up buying, it will be one of these four. They are not named here because the trade-off is in the kind, not in the brand, and the trade-off is the part worth thinking about.

Desktop software you install

You buy it, it runs on your machine, the file is yours. It keeps working when the vendor has a bad year.

The cost is that it is tied to that machine and that operating system, and somebody has to keep it current.

A cloud subscription

Somebody else runs it. Nothing to install, it works on every device, bank feeds tend to be better because the vendor maintains them.

The cost is that your records live on their servers, under their terms, for as long as you keep paying.

Open source you host

Free, and nobody can take it away. Usually a ledger or a budgeting tool, built well by people who wanted it themselves.

The cost is that you are the support desk, and these tools rarely go beyond recording what happened.

Adviser platforms

What an advice firm buys: a back office to hold the clients, a cashflow tool to do the planning, and something to produce compliance reporting.

The cost is that it is usually three or four products, bought separately and connected together.

WealthHorizon is the third kind with the ambition of the first and fourth. You install it on your own hardware, every edition, so your records never leave your building. It goes past recording what happened into long-term planning, tax and inheritance tax. And the Advisory edition puts the client book, the planning and the Consumer Duty evidence in one system rather than three. Whether that combination is worth the work of running it yourself is the question to answer, and it is a fair question to answer with no.

What WealthHorizon does not do

These are reasons to buy something else. Every one is checkable against this site or the product itself, which is more than we could honestly say about anybody else’s.

  • Bank feeds are not in production The Open Banking integration is built and works against the provider’s sandbox. Live use needs the regulatory permissions and provider onboarding that any account information service needs, and we do not have them. If you want transactions to arrive on their own today, this is the wrong product. It is on the limitations list as well.
  • One broker connection, and it is read-only Trading 212 is built and connectable. Saxo is at design stage and nothing else exists. Everything else arrives by file import, which the application does well and in a lot of formats, but it is still you doing it.
  • No integrations with adviser platforms There is no link to any back-office system, platform or provider feed. A firm with hundreds of clients should assume data arrives by import, and should price that work in before anything else.
  • You have to run it Every edition installs on your own hardware. That is the point of it and it is also a cost: somebody installs it, backs it up, keeps it patched and keeps the machine alive. We will not take that away from you by running it for you, because the moment we hold your records the main reason to use this is gone.
  • The planner does not model everything It is deliberately not a general-purpose modeller. It will not tell you which option to take, it holds every rate and allowance as reference data rather than guessing at a case it has no rules for, and it is explicit that cross-border pension and inheritance tax questions need a qualified specialist. What it does model is set out in full.
  • No independent accessibility audit We build to the WCAG 2.2 criteria for keyboard operability, visible focus and target size, and we test them in the browser suite. We have not commissioned a third-party audit and we claim no formal conformance level. A firm whose procurement needs one should ask before assuming.
  • No native mobile application There is no iPhone or Android app. The web application is built for phones and tablets, and will read offline once you have looked at something, but it is a web application.
  • It is new, and we are small Muneris Management Ltd is a small company and WealthHorizon has not been in the market for years. If buying from an established vendor with a support desk, a user group and a published roadmap matters to you, that is a good reason to buy from one.

The questions worth asking any of them

Including us. These are the ones where the answer tends to decide it, and where the answer is often not on the front page.

  • Where do my records physically live, and what happens to them if I stop paying?
  • Can I get everything out, in a format something else can read, without asking anyone?
  • Which countries does it actually work in? Several well-known products are sold only in North America, and bank connections are usually country by country.
  • More than one currency, properly? Not just a display setting — does each entry keep the rate and date it was converted at, so last year’s figures do not move when today’s rate does?
  • More than one tax jurisdiction in the same household?
  • Does it project forward, or only record what happened? Most personal finance software does the second.
  • If it projects, what tax does it apply — one flat rate, or the real bands for the right year and country?
  • Does it do inheritance tax at all, and for every year of the plan or only today?
  • For a firm: how many separate products is this, who connects them, and where does the Consumer Duty evidence come from?

Our answers to all of those are on this site, in the edition comparison, the planning page and how it is built. There is also a longer, feature-by-feature comparison with Microsoft Money, which we can make because that product has not changed since 2009 and we read its files.
