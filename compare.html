What is in each edition

Free and Standard hold exactly the same features. The difference is three numbers: Free stops at 30 accounts, 1,000 transactions and 5 MB of storage, and Standard does not. Pro adds everything to do with planning ahead, tax and forecasting. The Advisory edition contains all of Pro, plus the supervision and compliance layer described in The Advisory.

About the storage limit. It counts your records and any documents you file against an account, together. If you do not attach documents you will reach the transaction limit first; if you do, storage is what you will reach first — a few scanned statements go further than a year of entries. The limit belongs to the edition rather than to the hosting, so it is the same figure whether we run it or you run it on your own machine, and WealthHorizon tells you which limit you are approaching rather than refusing a save without saying why.

The Advisory edition contains everything in the Pro column for every client, and adds the supervision, Consumer Duty and record-keeping layer for the firm. It is described in full in The Advisory.

Coming from Microsoft Money

WealthHorizon compared with Microsoft Money

Microsoft withdrew Money from sale in 2009 and switched off its online services in 2011. The free “Sunset” build still runs on Windows, offline and unsupported, which is why a lot of people are still using it — it does the job and there has been nothing quite like it since.

The table below is an honest account of what carries over and what does not. Where Money did something well, that is said. WealthHorizon was written to be the same tool for the same job, not a different one with the same name.

Microsoft Money and WealthHorizon, feature by feature.
